About the project

Why we built EduMap

A short brief on the problem, the solution, and where this prototype goes next.

The problem

Thousands of schools across Tanzania, especially in rural wards operate with too few teachers, no reliable water source, and classrooms built for half the students they hold.

NGOs and government programs want to help, but there is no single, honest picture of which schools need what, so support tends to cluster around the schools that are already easiest to reach.

What EduMap is

A web platform that plots underrepresented schools on a map and attaches each one to concrete indicators: student-to-teacher ratio, class size, water and electricity access, and sanitation.

It is not a fundraising platform itself, it is the missing layer of visibility that makes existing donor and government programs more precise.

"The schools that most need help are usually the hardest to find information about." The observation EduMap started from

Well-connected urban schools already have parent associations, alumni networks, and visibility. Rural and under-resourced schools rarely have anyone advocating for them online. EduMap tries to close that gap with data instead of goodwill alone.

Prospectus: Where this goes next

  • Phase 1

    Verified data pipeline

    Partner with regional education officers to update school records, instead of manual admin entry.

  • Phase 2

    Donor accounts

    Let NGOs save shortlists, message schools, and track pledges over time.

  • Phase 3

    Progress tracking

    Before/after indicators once a school receives support, so impact is measurable.

  • Phase 4

    Mobile-first data entry

    A lightweight form teachers or officers can fill from a basic phone in low-connectivity areas.
